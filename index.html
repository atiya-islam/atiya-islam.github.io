Research

Physicians’ Location Choice and Access to Healthcare: The Case of Dobbs Job Market Paper

This paper studies how practice location-level regulation changes the geographic sorting of physicians across local markets. I use the variation in state-level measures of the legal environment resulting from the SCOTUS landmark decision to repeal the federal right to abortion in Dobbs v. Jackson Women’s Health Organization and combine it with longitudinal physician practice-location data to estimate a discrete-choice model of OB/GYN physician location. The analysis separates the decision to move from the choice of destination, allowing the legal environment to affect where physicians sort even when aggregate mobility responds weakly. I then use the estimated choice model to quantify how alternative legal environments reallocate physicians across locations and change access to obstetric and gynecologic care.

Role of Information on Physicians’ Prescription Choice: Evidence from FDA Accelerated Approval

This project studies how physicians adjust prescribing when the FDA Accelerated Approval program changes the information available about a drug’s clinical value, with a focus on how information frictions shape treatment choice in pharmaceutical markets.

Energy Transition in the Context of the First Market-Based Cap-and-Trade Program in the U.S.
with Emily Pakhtigian

This project examines how electric-generating plants adjust operation, generation, and fuel use in response to carbon-pricing incentives under the Regional Greenhouse Gas Initiative.
