I am a researcher in industrial organization and health economics, with additional interests in applied microeconomics and environmental and energy economics. My research primarily focuses on how regulation, information, and market incentives shape individual and firm behavior and the allocation of resources, particularly in healthcare markets.

My job market paper, “Physicians’ Location Choice and Access to Healthcare: The Case of Dobbs,” studies how practice location-level regulation changes the geographic sorting of physicians across local markets. I use variation in state-level measures of the legal environment resulting from the SCOTUS decision in Dobbs v. Jackson Women’s Health Organization and combine it with longitudinal physician practice-location data to estimate a discrete-choice model of OB/GYN physician location. The analysis separates the decision to move from the choice of destination, allowing the legal environment to affect where physicians sort even when aggregate mobility responds weakly. I then use the estimated choice model to quantify how alternative legal environments reallocate physicians across locations and change access to obstetric and gynecologic care.

My other work examines how physicians adjust prescribing when the FDA Accelerated Approval program changes information about a drug’s clinical value, and how electric-generating plants adjust operation, generation, and fuel use in response to carbon-pricing incentives under the Regional Greenhouse Gas Initiative.
