I am a Ph.D. candidate in Economics at The Pennsylvania State University. My research is in industrial organization and health economics, with additional work in applied microeconomics and environmental and energy economics. I study how regulation and information affect the choices of firms and professionals, and how those choices shape the allocation of resources across markets.

My job market paper, “Physicians’ Location Choice and Access to Healthcare: The Case of Dobbs,” studies how the legal environment affects where OB/GYN physicians choose to practice. I combine longitudinal physician practice-location data with a state-by-year measure of the legal environment and estimate a discrete-choice model of annual location choice. A one-standard-deviation increase in permissiveness raises the relative odds that a county is chosen as a destination by about 6 percent. The paper uses the model to study how alternative legal environments change the geographic distribution of physicians and access to care.

My other work studies how physicians respond to new information in pharmaceutical markets and how electric generating plants adjust production, capacity, and fuel use in response to carbon-pricing incentives.

Research

Physicians’ Location Choice and Access to Healthcare: The Case of Dobbs Job Market Paper

This paper studies how the legal environment affects where OB/GYN physicians choose to practice. I combine longitudinal physician practice-location data with a state-by-year measure of the legal environment and use reduced-form evidence together with a discrete-choice model of annual location choice. The main adjustment occurs through destination choice: a one-standard-deviation increase in permissiveness raises the relative odds that a county is chosen as a destination by about 6 percent. I use the estimated model to study how alternative legal environments change the geographic allocation of physicians and access to care.

Role of Information on Physicians’ Prescription Choice: Evidence from FDA Accelerated Approval

This project studies how physicians respond when information about a drug changes after market entry. I compile a novel dataset on approval and post-approval information, combine it with claims data, and use event-study and related causal designs to study how prescribing responds to new evidence.

Energy Transition in the Context of the First Market-Based Cap-and-Trade Program in the U.S.
with Emily Pakhtigian

This project studies how electric-generating plants respond to carbon-pricing incentives under the Regional Greenhouse Gas Initiative. We build plant-level panels from EIA 860, EIA 923, eGRID, and electricity-market data and study changes in generation, utilization, capacity, fuel use, and plant exit.
